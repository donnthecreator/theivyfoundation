450 Bikes business model

The hardest part of owning a dirt bike is finding somewhere legal to ride. Alabama has a lot of private land, and plenty of it is sitting unused. 450 Bikes connects the two.

How it makes money

  • Booking commission: 15% of rider fees.
  • Rider booking fee: $3 per rider per day.
  • 450 Bikes Pro ($9.99/mo): no booking fees, early access to new land, offline GPS trail maps.
  • Repair upcharges: riders add safety checks, flat coverage, trailside recovery, chain service, washes and tool rental at checkout. 450 Bikes keeps 20% and the mechanic gets 80%.
  • Insurance referral: commission from the liability insurance partner on each policy.
  • Later: group events, private race days, local shop ads.

Example: 400 bookings a month at $24.75 is about $9,900 a month in platform revenue. If 1 in 3 bookings adds $60 in repairs, that's another $1,600 a month for 450 Bikes and $6,400 for mechanics.

Real risks to solve first

  • Liability. Alabama has recreational use laws that limit landowner liability, but charging a fee can change that protection. Talk to an attorney and an insurance agent before launch.
  • Landowner trust. Rider ratings, damage deposits and ID verification.
  • Weather. Rain closes trails. Auto-cancel and refund when the owner closes for weather.
  • Neighbors and noise. Quiet hours and sound limits are part of every listing.
